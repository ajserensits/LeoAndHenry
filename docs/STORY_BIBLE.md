# Story Bible

## The heart of the game

Leo and Henry are brothers. Leo is the older, rounder golden-brown dog, but he is frightened by almost everything. Henry is about a year younger, black and white, alert, and naturally protective of Leo.

Leo still steals Henry's toys, treats, meals, and interesting little treasures. That mischief starts the chase, but Leo is never the villain. When an ordinary household event becomes scary, Henry stops worrying about the stolen item and helps his brother feel safe. Their relationship should always land on affection, comedy, and reassurance.

The recurring emotional pattern is:

1. Leo mischievously takes something belonging to Henry.
2. Henry follows Leo's trail.
3. A harmless but intimidating event frightens Leo.
4. Henry helps, protects, guides, or comforts him.
5. The brothers recover or share the item and return safely to Mom.

## Character canon

### Leo

- Small, very round, fluffy, and golden brown with a cream muzzle and chest.
- Older brother, despite behaving like the one who needs looking after.
- Loves food, toys, bugs, cozy hiding places, and stealing Henry's belongings.
- Afraid of vacuums, thunder, lawnmowers, plastic bags, cans, pools, strangers at the door, and losing sight of Mom.
- Expressive reactions range from suspicious side-eye to enormous startled eyes.
- Never mean-spirited. His thefts are impulsive, silly, and forgivable.

### Henry

- Small black-and-white dog with pointed ears, cream eyebrow markings, a white muzzle and chest, and large brown eyes.
- About a year younger than Leo.
- Brave, attentive, patient, and protective.
- Usually the active hero: he finds the route, clears the danger, locates Mom, or stays beside Leo until the frightening thing passes.
- May be exasperated about his missing treat, but never abandons Leo.

### Mom

- The dogs' emotional home base.
- Usually represented indirectly through a voice, footsteps, a silhouette, a familiar object, or the dogs' reaction.
- Finding Mom can itself be a story goal.
- Should feel warm and safe, not like a faceless quest dispenser.

## Tone rules

- Cozy, funny, and affectionate rather than dramatic or threatening.
- The “enemies” are normal objects and events seen from Leo's nervous perspective.
- Henry solves problems through care, cleverness, and companionship—not violence.
- Failure should never punish the player with lost lives. The game may restart a puzzle or offer help.
- Story text should be short enough to read comfortably on an iPhone.
- Endings should include a small brotherly gesture: sharing, leaning together, returning an item, or Henry staying close.

## Proposed opening story arc

The exact titles and level counts remain editable. Beats 3, 8, and 10 reflect specifically requested story moments.

|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Bug in the Beans | Leo takes Henry's red plush bug | Strange rustling in the vegetable patch | Henry follows the trail and shows Leo it is only friendly garden movement |
| 2 | The Vacuum Awakens | Leo grabs a biscuit during cleanup | The vacuum starts | Henry finds a quiet route into the garden and waits with Leo |
| 3 | Under the Bed | Leo runs off with Henry's toy | A loud household sound begins | Leo hides under the bed; Henry crawls close and coaxes him out when it is safe |
| 4 | Thunder over the Tomatoes | Leo takes the cozy blanket spot | A thunderstorm arrives | Henry gathers comforting objects and stays beside Leo through the storm |
| 5 | The Lawnmower Loop | Leo steals the outdoor ball | The lawnmower starts nearby | Henry leads Leo along the quiet side of the yard and back toward Mom |
| 6 | Bags, Cans, and Courage | Leo investigates Henry's snack | A plastic bag flutters and cans clatter | Henry clears the noisy path one object at a time |

## Chapter structure

Each chapter should have a consistent location, palette, backdrop, musical identity, and map treatment. Individual levels can change the objective and board obstacles while still feeling like part of the same place.

A chapter should generally contain:

- An opening illustration or very short story panel.
- A map with visible forward progress.
- Several puzzle objective types, introduced gradually.
- One or two recurring environmental complications.
- A final story payoff and a permanent garden or home decoration.

## Continuity guardrails

- Henry helps Leo whenever possible.
- Leo may steal Henry's things, but Henry's care matters more than getting them back.
- Do not portray Leo as malicious, selfish, or cowardly.
- Do not portray Henry as a stern authority figure.
- Keep the dogs physically small; their world feels large from their perspective.
- The peanut-butter pad is a rubber pad smeared with peanut butter and is a birthday tradition.
- Leo goes under the bed in Beat 3.
- Beat 8 centers on Mom wanting Leo outside by the pool; Leo dislikes the pool.
